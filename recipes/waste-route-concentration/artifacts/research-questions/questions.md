# Asked, answered, open

## Answered

**Is anything in this register consolidating?** Yes, one route. Deep-well injection holds
its tonnage (21.6M to 20.6M tons) while its record count halves and its firm count goes 46
to 31. Every other route's record count moves opposite to its tonnage, which is filing
granularity rather than activity.

**Which route is most concentrated?** Other treatment, Gini 0.98, because one firm is 86%
of it. **Not** deep-well injection, which has the fewest firms in the register and the
lowest Gini, 0.63. A first pass answered deep-well and was measuring average firm size.

**Has concentration increased since 2001?** On the all-firms Gini, no, in any route. On a
fixed count of each route's largest thirty firms, yes, in seven of eight. Firm counts fell
34–70% across the register and the exits come off the bottom of the distribution.

**Does a metal price explain recovery throughput?** No. Within-plant differences against
each plant's own metal: rho −0.002 at p 0.99 for lead plants over 53 intervals, powered to
detect 0.20. A levels version of the same panel gave −0.24 at p 0.009 and was a spurious
regression — both series trend.

## Partial

**What decides a ton's fate, if not price?** Held-out, per record: the handling facility
predicts 86% of it but is circular; location 55%; the waste's own form code 33%. Underneath
that, a generator's vendor — chosen for a *different* waste — is worth 8 points of recovery
rate holding stream, industry and state constant. That is a mechanism, not a full account,
and the 8 points is what survived controls from a raw 17.

## Open

**Does a route's concentration track anything outside the register?** Cement production,
oil price, and state permitting counts are all public and none has been joined. The metals
price join failed; the prior should be that these do too, and saying so in advance is the
point of writing it down.

*What would close it:* USGS Data Series 140 publishes cement in the same form as the zinc
and lead series already tested, so the join is proven to work mechanically.

**What are the lead times?** Not answerable here. All 67 columns were surveyed and **not
one is a date** — the Biennial Report is annual quantities per two-year cycle. EPA's
e-Manifest system carries per-shipment dates and is a different source.

**What does disposal cost against recovery, per ton, per state?** Not in the register.
Without it, a geographic gap — one state burying 168k tons with no recovery plant while a
neighbour runs 515k tons of capacity — cannot be distinguished from freight being correctly
priced.

*What would close it:* EREF publishes tipping fees at state level. Whether they join on
anything here is a screen, not an assumption.

**Is the site-to-owner normalisation good enough for a rank claim?** It collapses Clean
Harbors' 21 sites correctly and changed no category's rank. It has not been tested against
an external ownership source, so it is used for counting and not for naming a market leader
beyond the one case (Ascend) checked by hand.
