# Questions this recipe asks

## What the source is

**Hugging Face's model listings and its curated daily-papers feed.** Models carry
`arxiv:` tags naming papers they implement; the papers feed carries upvotes, GitHub
repo and star counts, AI-extracted keywords, and the submitting organisation.

The feed was thought to destroy its own history. It half does: **upvote trajectories
are gone forever, but membership is recoverable** via `?date=YYYY-MM-DD` back to
2023-05-04. One backfill run recovered 865 days — 17,534 papers, 63% with a star
count, 98% with keywords.

*Who has a stake:* anyone deciding whether to chase a new technique · anyone building
a model-selection or trend tool · anyone who wants to know whether "this paper is
trending" predicts anything at all.

### Ancestry: working backwards from the artifact

Proposed 2026-09-14: instead of asking which papers become models, start at a shipped
model, take the papers it cites, then the papers *those* cite, and keep walking — a
lineage trace rather than an attention measure.

## Known before starting

* **The stub's headline examples were wrong**, and checking them is what produced
  Q2 and Q3. "SigLIP at 10 adopters" is Google shipping nine of its own models.
* **Two zero results in this recipe were my own window, not the world.** The
  daily-feed join looked impossible until backfilled; the lag looked like 39 months
  until anchored on `createdAt`. Both times the data was there.
* **Three published answers here have since been retracted, and all three failed the
  same way** — a statistic was read as being about the subject when it was about the
  measurement.
  * **Q29, the dead topics.** `llms`, `gpt-4` and `NeRF` did not fade. They are all one
    word, and on 2025-12-25 the extractor nearly halved how many single-word keywords it
    emits. The original answer even supplied a mechanism — dead versus ambient — which
    made a measurement artifact sound like a finding about research.
  * **Q23, diffusion speed by field.** "Embeddings ship in 8 months, vision in 18" was
    the median over a mixture of papers that precede their models and papers that
    document one already shipped. Once separated, every field waits 20-25 months.
  * **Q2, model-tag counts.** Counting models counts release cadence; 32 of YaRN's 34
    are one company.

  The pattern worth carrying: **before believing a difference between groups, check
  whether the groups differ in how they were measured.** In all three cases the
  corrected answer was smaller and more useful than the original.

## The prescriptive set (2026-09-14, revised 2026-09-14)

Charts built to answer "what should I do" rather than "what is the case". Every one
carries its own failure mode in the footer, because a prescription that hides its
caveat is worse than a description.

**Fourteen were designed; twenty are on disk — nineteen of them prescriptive.** The fourteen were consolidated — several
prescriptions turned out to be one chart — and the table was left advertising filenames
that do not exist. Built and unbuilt are now separated. Nine charts were
added on 2026-09-14: six covering the funding questions and three of the designed set,
then five more when every answered question was given a figure — and one of those,
`15-ancestry-cliff`, retracted a fourth answer while being drawn. The remaining
unbuilt rows are kept because the prescription and the catch are the work; the drawing
is not.

**`11-instrument` is filed separately, in the method appendix below.** It is a control,
not a prescription: it says whether a number can be trusted, not what anyone should do
about research. It is worth keeping only because it invalidated Q29.

### Method appendix — charts about the data, not about research

These answer "can this number be trusted", not "what should I do". They are kept out of
the prescriptive set on purpose: a reader looking for a decision about research should
not have to step over them, and their value is entirely in what they invalidate
elsewhere. `11-instrument` earns its place only because it retracted Q29.

| file | what it is for | what it invalidated |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `11-instrument` | An undeclared extractor change on 2025-12-25 nearly halves single-word keyword emission (0.25 -> 0.14 of every list). Located by GMM on shape features with the declared model held out of the fit | **Q29 entirely** — `llms`, `gpt-4` and `NeRF` are all one word, flat for ten quarters, and collapse in the quarter holding the boundary. Also verifies Q27's RL rise as within-regime, and puts a haircut on the multi-word risers |

**The method was heavier than the finding needed.** The whole result reduces to one
derived quantity — single-word share over time — which steps visibly in a plain line
plot with no clustering at all. Three of the five features clustered on were noise. The
GMM bought a date and a pointer to *which* feature mattered; it did not find the
mechanism. Anyone repeating this should plot a handful of shape statistics of the
generator's output over time and look for steps before reaching for a mixture model.
