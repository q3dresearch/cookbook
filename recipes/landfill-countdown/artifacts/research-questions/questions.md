# Asked, answered, open

## Answered

**Is America running out of landfill room?** No, nationally — permitted room rose from 14.5bn
to 14.8bn tons across the interval, after burying about a billion. Yes, in eight states whose
median site has under twenty years.

**Do landfills run out by filling up or by taking more each year?** By filling up. Acceptance
rate is close to flat; remaining volume does the work.

**Is new capacity going where it is needed?** Yes. 1.3bn tons of new permitted capacity, and
it went disproportionately to the states with the shortest clocks.

**Who owns the room?** Two companies own half of it. The 510 single-site owners hold 33%
between them, median 3.3M tons each — a different industry, studied separately.

**Does a closing landfill fill up its neighbours?** No detectable effect at any radius tested.

**Are abandoned sites controlled?** It depends on size, not on closure era — which is the
opposite of the intuition that older means less regulated.

## Open

**Does the countdown predict closure?** Not testable with two editions three years apart. It
needs a third edition, and the only way to get one is to capture the next publication before
it is overwritten.

*What would close it:* a capture of the next LMOP edition. This is a standing reason to watch
the path rather than a research question.

**What does disposal cost, and does scarcity price?** Not in the register. EREF publishes
tipping fees at state level; whether they join on anything here is a screen, not an
assumption.

**Where does the waste actually go?** The register records a site's own intake, not shipments,
so interstate flow is invisible. The per-capita spread (New Jersey 6 tons a head, Nevada 385)
strongly implies flow, and this file cannot see it.

**Is the operator's own closure-year estimate better than the arithmetic?** They disagree, and
the recipe reports both without adjudicating. Settling it needs outcomes, which needs a third
edition.
